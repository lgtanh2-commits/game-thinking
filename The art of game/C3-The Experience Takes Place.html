Luận Điểm Cốt Lõi

Venue là gì và tại sao nó quan trọng hơn platform?

Vị Trí Của Chương 3 Trong Toàn Bộ Framework

Schell xây dựng một chuỗi logic xuyên suốt các chương đầu: Designer tạo ra Experience (Ch.2) → Experience đó xảy ra ở đâu đó (Ch.3) → Experience đó phát sinh từ một Game (Ch.4). Chương 3 đặt ra một bối cảnh quan trọng: trước khi thiết kế game, designer phải hiểu không gian vật lý và xã hội mà game sẽ được chơi.


Figure 3.1 trong sách minh họa điều này: Designer → (qua sợi dây vô hình) → Experience → (trong) → Venue. Không gian chứa đựng experience không phải là chi tiết phụ — nó là một lực lượng thiết kế quan trọng.

Venue Là Gì? Định Nghĩa Của Schell

Venue là những nơi trong cuộc sống của chúng ta mà chúng ta dùng các công nghệ đó — không phải technology, mà là loại không gian: "the places in our lives that we use those technologies." Hearth, workbench, reading nook, gaming table — những nơi này tồn tại từ trước khi có gaming và sẽ tiếp tục tồn tại sau khi mọi technology hiện tại đã lỗi thời.

The Shifting Sands of Platform

Tại sao debate về platform là sai hướng — và đúng hướng là gì

"It takes a bit of a mental shift to look past the technologies we use and instead to see the patterns of how we use them, but it is a good exercise, as it helps to give us insight about the past, present, and future of gameplay."
— Jesse Schell, Ch. 3, p. 26

Lịch Sử Nói Gì

Schell nhắc lại pattern lặp đi lặp lại trong lịch sử gaming và media: Television mostly replaced radio, nhưng không replace movies. Arcade gaming was replaced by PC gaming, which was replaced by console gaming, but then PC gaming came back, and mobile and tablet gaming rose up. Mỗi khi một technology mới thành công, người ta kết luận: "cái cũ sẽ chết." Và mỗi lần, kết luận đó đều sai — hoặc chỉ đúng một phần.


Lý do? Vì chúng ta nhìn vào technology (ephemeral, thay đổi liên tục) thay vì nhìn vào venue (persistent, tồn tại qua nhiều millennia). Hearth đã tồn tại từ khi con người kiểm soát được lửa — hơn 1 triệu năm trước. TV thay thế fireplace về mặt vật lý, nhưng the hearth as a venue — nơi gia đình tụ họp quanh ánh sáng và câu chuyện — vẫn còn đó.

Tốc độ thay đổi Nhanh — thay đổi mỗi vài năm (PC → console → mobile → cloud → VR...) Chậm — tồn tại qua hàng trăm đến hàng nghìn năm
Câu hỏi trọng tâm "Game này chạy trên thiết bị gì?" "Game này được chơi ở đâu, trong bối cảnh xã hội nào?"

Key Insight: Tại Sao Chúng Ta Mắc Bẫy Platform Thinking

Technology Is Shiny

Technologies are "new and shiny" — naturally grab attention. Venues are so familiar as to be invisible.

Recency Bias

Human nature: "when something is successful, it will remain that way forever." But it isn't the case.

Venue Persistence

The factors making specific technologies come and go are "as old and persistent as humanity itself."

Design Advantage

Seeing venue patterns gives insight into "past, present, AND future of gameplay" — a huge design advantage.

Hệ Thống Không Phải Perfect — Và Đó Là Chủ Đích

Schell thẳng thắn: "It's not a perfect system — it has both gaps and overlaps." Bowling alley thuộc về đâu? Casino? Restaurant có pinball? Không có câu trả lời rõ ràng. Nhưng đó không phải vấn đề — mục đích không phải là taxonomy hoàn hảo mà là một công cụ tư duy giúp designer nhìn past platforms để thấy venue properties.

Private Venues

Ba venue trong căn nhà — mỗi venue tạo ra một loại experience khác nhau

Tại Sao Privacy Quan Trọng Với Gaming

Schell giải thích: "Gameplay is often aided by privacy. To take the risk of immersing yourself in a fantasy world, we like to be in a safe place, either alone or surrounded by people we know and trust." Privacy không phải là luxury — nó là điều kiện tâm lý cho phép người chơi đủ vulnerable để fully immerse vào game world.

Private Venue #1

The Hearth

Nguồn gốc: Fire defined humanity — warmth, safety, cooking, social gathering. Controlling fire changed us "culturally, psychologically, and physically." Anthropologists theorize that trance-like staring at fire may be an evolved behavior — a survival advantage.

Ngày nay: TV screen replaced fireplace — right size, gives light in darkness, flickers similarly, tells its own stories. Homes still built with fireplaces because it "feels wrong" not to have one.

Game Design Implication: Hearth games work best for multiple people — either playing together OR fun to watch. Nintendo's Wii was perfect hearth hardware: whole family could play AND entertaining to watch. Wii U failed precisely because its separate handheld screen took it away from hearth dynamic.

Natural Fit: SingStar, Dance Central, party games, games fun to watch.

Family / Multi-player Fun to Watch Shared Screen TV / Console
Private Venue #2

The Workbench

Bản chất: Private space for working on hard problems — basement, quiet desk corner, sewing machine. Solitary, quiet, often a bit messy. Guests don't visit these spaces — it's the most private space in the home.

Tại Sao Máy Tính Tìm Đến Đây: Computers naturally found workbench — both work AND play on computer tend to be intense and solitary. Work of Warcraft và massive multiplayers stayed at the workbench because they require hours of focused play.

Steam's Secret: Valve's Steam succeeds at workbench precisely because Sony, Microsoft, Nintendo, Apple are focused on other venues — leaving Steam to own the workbench. VR head-mounted displays will likely also end up here.

Game Characteristics: Difficult, intense, hours of play at a time, online with like-minded others, technical depth.

Solo / Intense Long Sessions Lean Forward PC / Steam / VR
Private Venue #3

The Reading Nook

Bản chất: Reading chỉ phổ biến trong ~2000 năm gần đây nhưng đã ăn sâu vào văn hóa. Magic of sitting alone, letting text carry your mind away. Portable but preferred in specific quiet, comfortable spaces — bedroom, quiet chair away from TV.

Không Phải Hearth, Không Phải Workbench: Hearth is noisy and full of interruptions. Workbench is "lean forward" — too intense. Reading nook is the "lean back" activity — peaceful, relaxing, comfortable.

iPad Case Study: Game industry initially dismissed iPad — no phone convenience, no game console controls. But iPad captured reading nook perfectly: comfortable to hold in bed or on couch, peaceful and relaxing experience. Result: became a major gaming force. Tablet games are easier, simpler, more relaxing — exactly right for reading nook.

Lean Back Relaxing / Simple Interruptible Tablet / iPad

So Sánh Ba Private Venues: Game Design Implications

Hearth: Shared screen, social, broad audience (all ages), sessions can be short, visually impressive enough to be entertaining even when not playing.
Workbench: Individual screen, complex UI okay, long sessions, niche/dedicated audience, deep systems rewarded, players willing to pay more upfront.
Reading Nook: Handheld, one-thumb control possible, short bitable sessions, simple mechanics, free-to-play model often necessary, relaxing aesthetic preferred.

Public Venues

Ba venue ngoài gia đình — và thách thức độc đáo của interactivity trong public spaces

Rule of Public Venues: Give Them Something They Can't Get at Home

Schell trích dẫn nguyên tắc cốt lõi của location-based entertainment (LBE): "give them something they can't get at home." Whether running a tavern, theater, restaurant, brothel, theme park, or video arcade — the rule is the same. Public venues thành công khi họ cung cấp một experience không thể replicate trong không gian riêng tư.

Public Venue #1

The Theater

Magic Của Đám Đông: Something magical happens when a crowd focuses on a sequence of events simultaneously. We subconsciously sense how others feel about the performance — amplifying our own feelings. This is why TV shows have laugh tracks.

Tension Với Games: Games want to be a unique experience for each player. But theater depends on shared collective experience. These two things fundamentally conflict. When you try to make theatrical gaming (everyone in audience participates), "the joy of interactivity is watered down proportionately by the number of seats."

Current Status: No long-term theatrical gaming successes yet — either tiresome quickly OR theater too small to be profitable. Digital has made little impact here, but VR/AR may change this with clever solutions.

Collective Experience Hard for Games Shared Narrative
Public Venue #2

The Arena

Lịch Sử 4000+ Năm: Chariot racing, boxing, team sports — all take place in specialized competitive fields. Baseball diamonds, golf courses, soccer fields, tennis courts, horse tracks = arenas. Designed specifically for that type of gameplay.

Đặc Tính Cốt Lõi: Public record — who wins and who loses is witnessed publicly, not private. And when a crowd forms to watch, a theater forms around the arena — two venues in one!

Digital Arena Evolution: Multiplayer FPS = arena play in mind even if bodies are at workbench. BarCraft (watching StarCraft at bars), Twitch, YouTube gaming — virtual theaters forming around digital arenas. As mobile/AR advances, traditional arenas will become more digital.

Competitive Public Record Spectator-Friendly Esports / Streaming
Public Venue #3

The Museum

Bản Chất: Breaking from day-to-day life to examine exotic things → expand knowledge of world → return home with new perspective. Zoos, aquariums, shopping at unusual stores, sightseeing in cities = museum experiences.

Games Và Museums — Surprising Fit: Real museums increasingly incorporate gameplay to introduce visitors to exhibits. And visiting a video arcade has a very museum-like quality: "as you pass from game to game, trying each and thinking about which ones you like best."

Design Implication: Museum venue games benefit from: easy to pick up quickly, interesting to try briefly, designed for standalone play without prior knowledge, showcasing something "exotic" or novel.

Discovery-First Low Barrier Entry Novel / Exotic Arcade / LBE

Half Private / Half Public Venues

Ba venue nằm ở ranh giới — linh hoạt nhất trong hệ thống

Những venue này đặc biệt thú vị vì chúng tồn tại ở cả hai thế giới. Bạn có thể có gaming table ở nhà (khi có khách) nhưng cũng ở bar hay casino. Playground tồn tại trong sân nhà nhưng cũng ở công viên. Anywhere thì tất nhiên không cần một nơi cố định. Sự linh hoạt này tạo ra những design challenges và opportunities khác nhau.

Half & Half #1

The Gaming Table

Special Intimacy: Players sit "like gods over the toy world they control" — a face-to-face intensity unique to table play. You can see your opponents' faces, read tells, share the same physical space over the same game.

Private và Public: Mostly done at home when guests are over (semi-public) but also exists publicly: pool tables at bars, poker tables at casinos. This flexibility is key to its longevity.

Historical Fun Fact: The sandwich AND the sushi roll were both invented in the 18th century, by table gameplayers so obsessed with their games that they needed a way to eat without stopping play — in very different parts of the world.

Digital Gap: Board, card, dice games dominate this venue. Digital games haven't found a home there yet (due to reliance on vertical screens). But touch screens growing cheaper and larger → whole new world of digital gaming table possible.

Face-to-Face Intense / Social Tabletop-First Board / Card / Dice
Half & Half #2

The Playground

Largely Children's Domain: Adults forget that play is primarily children's territory. Children play differently: formally in arena-like spaces (organized sports) AND informally out-of-doors with friends (improvisational).

Definition: Any space where children gather for improvisational play — public park equipment, but also a backyard, a street, a vacant lot, a cave in the woods. Playground is defined by the activity and social context, not the specific location.

Industry Blind Spot: Video game industry has largely ignored playground play — not feasible to create games that work outdoors with rough-and-tumble play. But technology becoming more rugged and mobile → this will likely change.

Importance: Playground play is "an important part of [children's] development" — not a niche, but fundamental to childhood.

Improvisational Children / Outdoor Physical Underserved by Digital
Half & Half #3

Anywhere

Classic Anywhere Games: Crossword puzzles, Sudoku, Word Find — "play anywhere" games that fill spare minutes on a bus or at work. These games pre-date smartphones by decades.

Smartphone Revolution: Explosion of smartphone games has changed the anywhere venue forever — vastly richer set of games playable anywhere, anytime. But the fundamental venue properties remain the same.

Key Properties: Playable in tiny bites. Very simple interfaces and stories. Interruptible at any moment. Small screen size. Players generally unwilling to pay much upfront — prefer free if possible.

Design Constraint: These limitations are not bugs — they're features of the venue. Games that succeed in Anywhere are designed for interruption, not fighting against it.

Bite-Sized Interruptible Simple UI Free-to-Play

Venues Mixed and Matched

Sử dụng hệ thống venue như một design tool — không phải taxonomy

Các Câu Đố Thú Vị Của Schell

Schell đặt ra một số câu hỏi để minh họa rằng hệ thống có gaps và overlaps — và đó là điểm mấu chốt:


• "What venue am I in when I play a game of pinball at a restaurant?" (Museum? Arena? Gaming Table?)
• "What venue is a bowling alley?" (Arena? Somewhere public but also intimate?)
• "Is a casino a museum, an arena, a collection of table games, or something else entirely?"


Câu trả lời thường là: nhiều venues cùng một lúc. Đây không phải lỗi của hệ thống — đây là thực tế. Nhiều không gian cung cấp nhiều loại experience đồng thời.

Mục Đích Thực Sự Của Hệ Thống Venue

Schell nói rõ: "The important thing isn't to have a perfect taxonomy of venues; the important thing is to see past the games and platforms so that you are aware of the venues and their properties, because while games and technologies change constantly, venues change very little." Framework này là một lens để nhìn, không phải một rulebook để tuân theo.

Venue Properties Quan Trọng Với Design

⏱

Session Length

Anywhere = minutes. Reading Nook = 20-60 min. Workbench = hours. Hearth = flexible but social pacing.

Social Context

Hearth = family/friends watching. Workbench = solo or online strangers. Gaming Table = intimate face-to-face. Arena = public competition.

Interruption Tolerance

Anywhere = must handle interruption. Reading Nook = low interruption expected. Workbench = low. Hearth = moderate (social interruptions).

Willingness to Pay

Anywhere = prefer free. Workbench = willing to pay significant upfront. Hearth = depends on family budget. Arena = event tickets.

Screen & Controls

Hearth = large shared screen. Workbench = individual monitor. Reading Nook = handheld. Anywhere = phone-sized.

Cognitive Load

Workbench = high complexity welcomed. Hearth = moderate (accessible to all family). Reading Nook = low-medium. Anywhere = very low.

Lens #3: The Lens of the Venue

Lens #3 of 113
The Lens of the Venue
Venue định hình game — đừng design trong chân không
"The places that we play exert tremendous influence on the design of our games. To make sure you aren't designing in a vacuum, ask yourself these questions:"
1
What type of venue best suits the game I'm trying to create?
Ai sẽ chơi game này và ở đâu? Không gian nào trong cuộc sống của họ là phù hợp nhất? Hearth (family gaming?), Workbench (solo deep-dive?), Reading Nook (relaxing mobile?), Arena (competitive?)?
2
Does my venue have special properties that will influence my game?
Những đặc tính của venue đó là gì? Session length, interruption tolerance, social context, screen size, willingness to pay, cognitive load available? Những đặc tính này buộc bạn phải design như thế nào?
3
What elements of my game are in harmony with my venue? What elements are not?
Đối chiếu từng mechanic, UI decision, session structure, social feature với venue. Nếu một element không phù hợp với venue — ví dụ một complex MMO-style UI cho Anywhere game — đó là một red flag cần address.

Lens #3 Trong Thực Tế: Wii Success

Nintendo designed Wii explicitly for the Hearth venue: simple motion controls accessible to all ages, games fun to watch even when not playing, no complex controller configurations. Every element in harmony with the venue → massive commercial success.

Lens #3 Trong Thực Tế: Wii U Failure

Wii U's separate handheld screen moved focus from shared Hearth TV to individual handheld — shifting venue to Reading Nook, which already had iPad and 3DS. Elements no longer in harmony with the Hearth venue it was supposed to serve.

Further Reading: A Pattern Language — Christopher Alexander et al.

Schell recommends này như "an incredibly thought-provoking book about the relationship between humans and the spaces they live in." Alexander's work on how physical spaces shape human behavior và culture is highly relevant to venue-based game design thinking. (Schell sẽ reference lại trong Chapter 21: "Spaces.")

Tóm Tắt Chương 3 — Key Takeaways

Những concepts cần nhớ và áp dụng trong game design

Platforms Are Ephemeral, Venues Are Permanent

Technologies come and go. The Hearth, Workbench, Arena — these have existed for thousands of years and will persist. Design for venue, not platform.

The Hearth: Shared, Social, Watchable

Family room TV games. Must work for multiple people — playable together AND fun to watch. Wii succeeded; Wii U failed by violating this principle.

The Workbench: Intense, Solo, Deep

PC desk, long sessions, high complexity, dedicated players. Steam owns this. VR will likely live here. Willing to pay premium for depth.

The Reading Nook: Lean Back, Relax

Tablet/handheld, simple and calming. iPad captured this venue unexpectedly. Games here: easier, simpler, more relaxing than hearth or workbench.

Theater + Interactivity = Hard Problem

Collective experience dilutes individual interactivity. "Joy of interactivity watered down proportionately by number of seats." No long-term solutions yet.

Arena Creates Theater Around Itself

Competitive games attract spectators → theater forms around arena. Esports, BarCraft, Twitch = this ancient pattern applied to digital games.

Gaming Table: Face-to-Face Intensity

Unique intimacy. "Players sit like gods over the toy world they control." Sandwich + sushi roll invented here. Digital not yet found its place at this venue.

Anywhere: Design FOR Interruption

Bite-sized, simple, free-to-play. Smartphone revolution expanded this venue but didn't change its fundamental properties. Work with the constraints, not against them.

Lens #3: Audit Every Element vs. Venue

What venue? What are its special properties? What's in harmony, what isn't? This lens prevents designing in a vacuum and grounds decisions in real player context.
